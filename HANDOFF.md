### How the Fairy Realm works

- **Sanctums.** `generate_fairy_realm.py` builds `structure/fairy_sanctum.nbt` (33x41x41: a shrine on top, a spiral staircase, a domed hall with the frames) and a one-piece jigsaw structure whose template y 33 lands on the first free block above the ground (`start_height` -33 with `project_start_to_heightmap`), `random_spread` 56/24 chunks, `terrain_adaptation: none`. The processor list `fairy_sanctum` fills about one frame in eight with a pearl. There are no maps to them: `item/TransCrystalPearlItem` finds the nearest one with `ChunkGenerator#findNearestMapStructure` and throws an `entity/CrystalEye` at it, a `ThrowableItemProjectile` that steers like vanilla's eye of ender (12 blocks at a time, rising 8) and drops back as a pearl four times in five.
- **The portal.** `block/FairyPortalFrameBlock` takes a pearl (`useItemOn`), then `FairyRealm.tryOpenPortal` looks for a complete ring of twelve filled frames around a 3x3 hole (end portal layout) and fills it with `block/FairyPortalBlock`, which implements vanilla's `Portal` (`entity.setAsInsidePortal`), so travel works like the end portal for every entity. `FairyRealm.portalDestination` sends you to the Fairy Realm, remembering the portal you used (`ModAttachments.FAIRY_RETURN`), or from there back beside that portal.
- **The realm.** `dimension/fairy_realm.json` is a flat world of air whose one biome (`fairy_realm`) only places `FairyIslandFeature` islands (none within 96 blocks of 0,0). The dimension type copies the Trans Realm's (same timelines, so the same sky) with clouds at y 72, below the islands. The client treats it as "in the realm" for the heart clouds and mob colours. It has no intro (the owner found it annoying), and coming back from it doesn't replay the Trans Realm's.
- **The arena.** The first arrival (or any player arriving by other means, through `ServerEntityLevelChangeEvents`) places `structure/fairy_realm/arena_island.nbt` at (-36, 84, -36); the Trans Fairy isn't there yet. `FairyRealmState` (a level attachment) remembers that it's built, whether she's been beaten, and whether Maddie is gone. `ARENA_*` in the script and the constants in `world/FairyRealm` must agree. On peaceful the portal home opens at once.
- **The boss.** `entity/TransFairy` is a `Monster` that flies by setting its own velocity (`travel` is overridden) and runs a small state machine in `customServerAiStep`: hover (orbit around her home), volley (`TransMagicBolt`s), swoop, spikes (`entity/FairyCrystalSpike`, like evoker fangs), summon (trans endermen) and starfall, with three phases by health. The synced `ACTION` drives the model's poses. On death `FairyRealm.onFairyDefeated` opens the portal home; her loot table drops the Fairy Jar. She has no vanilla boss bar: `client/TransFairyBossBar` (a HUD element after vanilla's boss bars) finds the nearest fairy and draws her synced health as the flag, with a shimmer and sparkles. While her synced `INTRO` flag is set she only hovers and watches, can't be hurt, and the bar stays hidden.
- **Calling her: the ritual and the cutscene.** A Trans Crystal or Crystal Pearl thrown onto the `fairy_altar` (an `ItemEntity` above it, checked every 5 ticks) or used on it calls her through `FairyRealm.offerAtAltar`. Until Maddie is gone that starts `world/FairyCutscene`, a server-side timeline (not saved) whose public constants the client's camera follows too: START, LINE and END go to watching players as `FairyCutscenePayload`s (`client/FairyCutsceneOverlay` draws the subtitles, Maddie's name in pink and the fairy's in blue; `client/FairyCutsceneCamera` cuts between takes through `CameraMixin` (round 16; it used to fly one Catmull-Rom curve through key shots), switches to third person meanwhile and hides the HUD through Fabric's `HudElementRegistry.replaceElement`), the audience is held still (transient -100% movement speed and jump strength modifiers, `cutscene_hold`), Maddie's time door opens 8.5 blocks north of the altar (drawn by the camera class, `FairyCutsceneCamera#renderDoor`: a bar of light across the top, then the door unrolling downwards in a soft trans gradient, white in the middle to blue at the edges), a cutscene-only Maddie (`Maddie#actInCutscene`, no AI) steps through it, walks to her mark and says three lines, the fairy appears in intro mode with the old spawn burst (`FairyRealm.spawnFairy`) and says a line. Maddie calls her friends (round 15): two more doors open five blocks either side of hers (`HELP_PORTALS`), and a `SculkArcher` walks out of each. It's a `noSave` mob with no AI, drawn like a sculk person by `SculkArcherRenderer`, a `HumanoidMobRenderer` so the bow in its hand shows, held up with both arms while its synced `AIMING` flag is set. Each looses two arrows at the fairy: vanilla arrows from `EntityTypes.ARROW`, with `noPhysics`, no gravity and no damage, so they fly straight through anything and can't hurt anyone. The scene stops each one where it reaches her shield (`SHIELD_RADIUS`, 2.2 blocks from her middle) with a sound, sparks and a `SHIELD` payload, and the client draws the shield as a faint bubble in the flag's gradient that flares at each hit (`FairyCutsceneCamera#renderShield`). She strikes each archer with a bolt (`castAt`; each is left on one heart, with a fallback kill 15 ticks later), and their loot (`loot_table/entities/sculk_archer`: a worn bow, arrows, sculk gems, sometimes an echo shard) drops where they fall. Then she fires one `TransMagicBolt` at Maddie (`TransFairy#castAt`; Maddie is left on one heart so the bolt kills her, with a fallback kill 15 ticks later), `FairyRealmState.maddieGone` is set, the fairy turns to the player for two more lines, and the fight starts. From then on every Maddie hands her home over to Kira and discards herself on her next check (one in the Fairy Realm that isn't the actor just goes), and offerings call the fairy straight back. If nobody is left in the realm mid-scene (`endUnwatched`), it's called off before Maddie falls (she and the fairy go, and the offering comes back as a Trans Crystal on the altar); after that, the fairy is just left ready to fight. The scene is static state, so `SERVER_STOPPED` clears it (it used to carry over into the next singleplayer world and block the fairy there). On Peaceful she stays (her type doesn't opt out of `isAllowedInPeaceful`) but can't hurt anyone, and the arena says so.
- **Rendering.** `TransFairyModel` has two layer definitions with the same parts: the solid body and a glow layer (wings and wand star) that `TransFairyGlowLayer` draws translucent and full bright. The crystal spike is drawn full bright too. The Fairy Jar's light is part of the jar's block model (a glowing cube and two wing quads, `light_emission` 15, its texture cycling pink, white and blue): it used to be a block entity renderer, which never appeared in the owner's game through rounds 4 to 6 (cutout or translucent), for reasons not found.

### How the camps work

`generate_camps.py` reads 26.3's abandoned camp templates (the cherry grove version: 10 tents, 45 shared campsites and 4 cherry grove ones, fetched from misode/mcmeta's `26.3-data` branch into `tools/vanilla_extra/structures/abandoned_camp/`). 26.3 writes palettes as `id`/`properties` instead of `Name`/`Properties`; the script rewrites them in 26.2's format with data version 4903. Three things don't exist in 26.2: white wool stairs (our light blue, pink and white wool stairs, chosen by row so the tents are flag-striped), straw beds (trans beds) and cushion entities (our cushion blocks, one block above where the entity sat; their 16 colours fold into three). The loot tables are 26.3's with its camp and treasure maps swapped for trans items. A camp is a tent (the start pool) joined to one campsite; the tents' tree jigsaws grow `trans_cherry_tree_checked` or `trans_tree_bees_checked`. Jigsaw pieces are placed with a known shape, so fence and wall connections come from the templates and beds keep `heart=none`.

### How the mermaid ruins work

`generate_mermaid_ruins.py` builds three single-piece templates (court, arch, cottage) with the paving at template y 3 and foundation below it; the structure uses `project_start_to_heightmap: OCEAN_FLOOR_WG` with `start_height` -3, so the paving replaces the top block of the sea floor. Templates contain no air, so the sea stays in every gap, and all waterloggable blocks are saved un-waterlogged: jigsaw placement's default `liquid_settings` (`apply_waterlogging`) waterlogs them wherever the sea already was, so a ruin poking out of a shallow sea doesn't leak water. The cottage's room is filled with water blocks so a slope can't bury it. The processor list cracks bricks, wears diorite and prismarine and knocks the odd block out at random. Biomes: Trans Ocean, Deep Trans Ocean, Pastel Reef.

### How sitting works

`FurnitureBlock` takes an optional seat height (pixels). Using a seat with an empty hand calls `entity/Seat.sit`: it spawns an invisible `Seat` entity on the seat's surface and the player rides it (one per block). The seat discards itself as soon as nobody rides it or the block under it stops being a seat. Players sit with their hips 0.6 above their feet (the player's vehicle attachment), so the seat entity goes exactly at the surface.

### How the plushes work

Village houses carry an invisible `plush_spot` block (a corner of the room, facing in; `generate_villages.py` puts one in 22 of the 36 house pieces). It has a ticking block entity. On its first tick the server looks up the trans village whose piece it sits in, then checks the realm level's `PlushLedger` (a persistent Fabric attachment). If that village has no plush yet, the spot turns into one, choosing among the cats handed out least so far, and the village is recorded. Otherwise the spot turns into air. So every village gets exactly one plush, in whichever house loads first, and the first nine villages give nine different cats. The ledger stores indexes into `ModBlocks.PLUSHES`: only ever append to that list.

### How the Egg House is built

`generate_egg_house.py` reads the region files straight out of `Egg House!.zip` (a 1.16.5 world), copies the house, its garden, the trees and the flag, swaps materials (`RENAME`, `LEAVES`, beds become trans beds with their `heart` set), carves an island under it and writes `structure/egg_house_island.nbt`. The template keeps the save's data version (2586) so Minecraft's data fixers upgrade the item frames, the armour stand and the lectern books when it loads; block names are written in their current form. Chests and barrels get the `egg_house` loot table. Maddie stands on the ground floor. The structure is a one-piece jigsaw 45-60 blocks above the ground at its start, `random_spread` 48/24 chunks. The script also adds a rare treasure-map pool to `chests/trans_house`.

When the island is placed, the item frames may log "Hanging entity at invalid position" once: their saved block position points at the original world. Placement moves them to the right spot anyway.

### Kira

### Herobrine

`herobrine/Herobrine` (server) and `client/herobrine/` (client). He is never an entity. The server picks, for each player the host chose, when and where he stands. It sends that player alone a `HerobrineSightingPayload`, and their client draws him straight from his skin as custom geometry (`SkinFigure`: textured boxes laid out like the player model, `RenderTypes.entityCutout`, lit by the world's light where he stands, with a full-bright eyes texture over his face). So nothing that lists entities finds him: minimap radars like Xaero's, F3's entity count, hitboxes, the player list. Other players' clients are never told.

- **Settings** (`HerobrineSettings`, a persistent attachment on the Overworld, `ModAttachments.HEROBRINE`): on or off (off until saved), who sees him (other players / only me / everyone; "me" is whoever saved the settings), far away / behind you / either, day and night / only at night / only by day (daytime is when the day timeline's `monsters_burn` attribute is on), any weather / clear / rain / thunder, the Overworld only or anywhere, about every N minutes, how long he stays, how close you can get, gone when stared at, and a cave sound as he goes.
- **The secret command** `/tdsettings` (`Herobrine.COMMAND`) needs the singleplayer/LAN host (`MinecraftServer#isSingleplayerOwner`) or an operator; nobody else's game is even sent it. It opens `HerobrineScreen` (literal English labels, kept out of the lang file). "Save and show him now" saves and makes him appear at once to everyone who sees him, and tells the host how many it reached.
- **Sightings**: each eligible player gets one chance a second (on average one sighting every N minutes), never in their first minute online or within 30 seconds of the last one ending. A far sighting stands 8 to 44 blocks beyond the vanish distance, on the surface (or a cave floor near their level, if they're underground), with nothing solid between their eyes and his head. A behind sighting stands 3 to 6 blocks straight behind them.
- **Vanishing** is the client's (`HerobrineClient#tick`): when they come within the vanish distance, after 1.5 seconds of looking straight at him (within about 7 degrees, if "stared at" is on), 6 ticks after they turn to face one behind them, or when the time's up. The cave sound is `SoundEvents.AMBIENT_CAVE`, played only on their client.
- **Skin**: `tools/art/herobrine_skin.png` (the owner's `herobrineskin.png` from `main`), copied by `generate_textures.py` to `entity/herobrine/herobrine.png`, with `herobrine_eyes.png` made from its near-white face pixels.

### How the mobs work

- **Trans fish** extend vanilla's `Cod` (schooling, flopping, bucketing) with their own model and bucket item.
- **Trans endermen** extend `EnderMan` without the block-carrying goals. `EnderManMixin` swaps the portal particles in `aiStep` for light blue dust (`require = 0`); `handleEntityEvent(46)` makes the teleport burst. Their glowing eyes are a second, slightly bigger head drawn full bright (`TransEndermanModel.createEyesLayer`).
- **Pastel slimes** are `TamableAnimal`s that only move by hopping: `travel` is ignored on the ground except on the tick they jump, and `jumpFromGround` pushes them towards where they're going. `squish` drives the squash-and-stretch in the model; a second model draws the translucent jelly coat. The **Bottled Fairy** saves its holder through Fabric's `ServerLivingEntityEvents.ALLOW_DEATH`, which fires just before vanilla's totem check (Fabric redirects the second `isDeadOrDying()` in `hurtServer`), so the item has no death protection component and none of the totem's green particles. `BottledFairy` checks the hands in vanilla's order (a totem in an earlier hand wins), swaps the bottle for an empty one, heals and buffs the holder, sends the sparkles and sounds from the server, and sends `FairyRescuePayload` so the saved player's client pops the Bottled Fairy up on screen like a totem. A slime keeps only a colour it was saved with: spawn eggs and `/summon` load it from data with no colour, so it rolls one on its first tick like a wild slime.

### How the wings work

Player movement is client side, so `WingsController` (client) reads the jump and sneak keys and changes the player's velocity: charge and launch, flap, softer glide, hover. It sends a `WingActionPayload` for each action. The server (`TransWings.handleAction`) checks the wings are worn, starts the glide after a launch (`tryToStartFallFlying`), plays sounds and particles, and relays a `WingFlapPayload` to players tracking the flyer so their client plays the flap animation. `RealmEvents` cancels fall and fly-into-wall damage for wearers.

The wings are an equippable chest item with the `glider` component and no equipment asset, so vanilla draws nothing. `AvatarRendererMixin` puts a `WingPose` on each player's render state (from `WingAnimations`, which eases the spread and times flaps per entity id), and `TransWingsLayer` (added to every `AvatarRenderer` through `LivingEntityRenderLayerRegistrationCallback`) draws `TransWingsModel` on the body. Since round 4 each wing is an arm bone, a hand bone and overlapping feather planes; folded, the feathers lie flat along the back without crossing the body, and a flap swings the whole wing up and down. Players wearing them don't show their cape.

### Clouds

Since 1.21.6 the cloud renderer only takes shapes from `clouds.png`; colours come from the `minecraft:visual/cloud_color` attribute. So the hearts keep coming from `HeartClouds` and the colours from the `trans_clouds` timeline, which multiplies the realm's white base colour through the flag's exact blue (`#5BCEFA`), pink (`#F5A9B8`), white and pink over five minutes. Paler tints washed out under shader packs. `extras/BSL_Trans_Realm.txt` is a BSL 10.1.8 preset that switches BSL to vanilla clouds (`CLOUDS=3`) so the hearts show, and the mod tints them (see "Shaders (Iris and BSL)"). The day timeline still dims it at night.

### Shaders (Iris and BSL)

Round 13 was written from the owner's own BSL 10.1.8 zip and Iris 1.11.4+mc26.2 jar (shared on `main`), read with `unzip` and `javap`. Nothing depends on Iris at build time: the hooks are optional `@Pseudo` mixins with string targets, `remap = false` and `require = 0`, and the API call is by reflection.

- **How a pack sees the world.** A pack's `block.properties` gives vanilla blocks (or states) numbers; Iris hands each block state's number to the shaders as `mc_Entity`. BSL's numbers (`block.AAABB`, see the head of its block.properties): 100s wave and get subsurface light (grass 100, flowers 101, tall plants 102/103, crops 104, leaves 105, vines 106), 150s glow (lava 153, fire 154, candles 158, ores 159), 200s are see-through (water 200, glass 201, nether portal 203: glowing, with depth), 250s are flags. BB is a light colour, only used with its Multicolored Blocklight option (off by default). Our blocks weren't listed, so they all got 0.
- **Twins** (`client/compat/ShaderCompat#addTwins`, from `IrisWorldRenderingSettingsMixin` at the head of Iris's `WorldRenderingSettings#setBlockStateIds(Object2IntMap<BlockState>)`). Iris builds that map once per pack load in `IrisRenderingPipeline#beginLevelRendering` (an `Object2IntLinkedOpenHashMap`, default -1). Before it stores it, each of our block states that the pack doesn't list gets the id of its vanilla twin's matching state (properties copied by name and value name). Twins come from `twinName`: an id table for blocks whose class doesn't say what they are (`pink_lava` → lava, `holy_water` → water, `sky_portal` → nether_portal because it's see-through, `fairy_jar` → glass, `trans_sea_lantern`, `ritual_crystal` → amethyst_cluster, `trans_lamp` → redstone_lamp, `pink_sculk_catalyst`), then the block's class (leaves, saplings, flowers, tall flowers, grass, bushes, seagrass, kelp, corals, cave vines, cane, sea pickles, amethyst-style clusters, torches and wall torches, lanterns, gel blocks, pink sculk), then `_ore` → diamond_ore and glass. Our blocks glow under a pack exactly where their vanilla twins do. Round 13 also gave glowstone's id to every lit state without a twin, so BSL drew them glowing on top of their block light; that fallback is gone. It logs how many states it filled ("Shader pack block ids: gave N block states..."). New blocks are covered by their class; add an id entry for anything special.
- **BSL's clouds.** With `CLOUDS=3` (vanilla) Iris draws the game's clouds with `gl_Color` = face shade × the game's `CloudColor` (`VanillaTransformer`'s cloud emulation), but BSL's `program/gbuffers_clouds.glsl` only keeps the colour's alpha. `IrisIncludeGraphMixin` hooks Iris's `IncludeGraph#readFile(Path)` (every pack file is read through it). For a file called `gbuffers_clouds.glsl` that still contains BSL's line `albedo.rgb = pow(albedo.rgb,vec3(2.2));` (and `CLOUD_BRIGHTNESS` and `varying vec4 color;`), `ShaderCompat#patchShaderFile` adds a line after it that multiplies by the vertex colour divided by its brightest channel (the hue without the face shade), in linear space. White clouds don't change; ours cycle through the flag. Nothing on disk is touched; another pack, or a BSL that no longer has that line, is left alone.
- **Glowing geometry** (`GlowGeometry`) draws with `RenderTypes.entityTranslucent` on a plain white texture again. Round 13 used `RenderTypes.beaconBeam`: Iris sends it to the pack's beacon beam program, which BSL draws as pure light ×4, and it came out far too bright. Glowing shapes are full bright without shaders and at light 13 with them (`SHADER_GLOW`); the cloud boat's lead isn't glowing and takes the light where it is (`lit`).
- **Light levels** (round 14), close to vanilla's (an amethyst block or gold block gives none, an amethyst cluster 5, an end portal frame 1, a nether portal 11, a torch 14): crystal ores and blocks, the pastel prism, star blooms, holy gold, gilded cloudcite and holy water give none; prism (crystal) clusters 5, like amethyst clusters; the Fairy Portal frame 1; the altar 5; a candle on a ritual stand 3; the ritual crystal 5, or 7 awake; the Sky Portal 11; the Fairy Jar 9; our chests 4; the trans torch 14. Block models have no `light_emission` faces: a full-bright face looks flat, with no shading, and a block that gives light already lights itself.
- **Glowing mobs.** BSL lights an entity at `(pow(x, 10) × 1.6 + x × 0.6)²` × the block light colour, about 1.86 at full light, so pale full-bright mobs come out flat white. `ShaderCompat.glowLight()` is 15 without shaders and 13 with a pack on (Iris's `IrisApi.getInstance().isShaderPackInUse()`, by reflection); wild fairies and angels use it, and the jar's fairy always uses the jar's 12.
- **What BSL does with our dimensions.** Its `dimension.properties` maps `minecraft:overworld *` to world0, so every modded dimension gets the Overworld programs (sun, shadows, BSL's sky). `SKY_VANILLA` makes BSL's sky and fog start from the game's `skyColor` and `fogColor` (our timelines), and `USE_FOG_COLOR` makes fog and the horizon use `fogColor` (without it, fog is sky-blue and the realm's pink fog is lost). Particles go through `gbuffers_textured`, lit by their light level: ours are full bright, so they glow.
- **The preset.** `extras/BSL_Trans_Realm.txt` must use values BSL offers: Iris quietly drops any other value for the option's default. `python3 tools/check_bsl_preset.py <BSL zip>` checks every line against the zip's `lib/settings.glsl`. Numbers must match the listed spelling exactly (`CG_TM=0.2`, not `0.20`).
- **Next, if wanted.** A Trans Realm-specific look would need BSL's `dimension.properties` to map `transdimension:trans_realm` to a world folder of its own: a modified pack (`IncludeGraph` can't add files), so it would have to be a patched copy of the owner's zip, kept out of the repo.

### The realm's own terrain and pink lava

- **Own terrain (round 12).** The Trans Realm used the overworld's noise router, so with the same seed its continents, mountains and caves lined up with the overworld's. Noises are seeded from their ids, so `generate_worldgen.py` (`generate_noise_settings`, `own_terrain`) builds the realm's noise settings from a copy of vanilla's overworld settings (`tools/vanilla_extra/templates/worldgen/overworld/`: `noise_settings.json` without its surface rule, 19 density functions, 35 noises) and points every density function and noise they use at our own copy, `transdimension:trans/<vanilla path>` (written under `worldgen/density_function/trans/` and `worldgen/noise/trans/`). The shapes are vanilla's; the landscape is new. The biome source reads the same router, so biomes follow the new terrain. Old worlds keep their generated chunks, so there's a seam where new chunks meet old ones.
- **Pink lava.** `NoiseBasedChunkGeneratorMixin` (round 5) only reaches the fluid picker's deep lava; vanilla's aquifers still put orange lava in some caves. `PinkLavaFeature` is the last feature of every biome (`steps[10]`, last in `FEATURE_RANK`, no placement modifiers, so once per chunk): it looks through each section that may hold lava (`maybeHas`) and swaps every lava block for pink lava of the same level. Lava that has to flow is marked for post-processing by vanilla, which ticks whatever fluid is there when the chunk is finished, so it flows as pink lava. The realm's springs were already pink (`spring_pink_lava`).

### How the pink deep dark and the Cloud Realm work

- **Pink sculk.** `pink_sculk_sensor`, `pink_sculk_shrieker` and `pink_sculk_vein` are vanilla's block classes registered under our ids; the sensor and shrieker share vanilla's block entity types (`ModBlocks.initialize` calls Fabric's `addValidBlock`), so they hear and shriek like vanilla's. Shriekers are only ever placed with `can_summon=false` (worldgen and players alike), so they never summon a vanilla warden or give darkness. Vanilla's catalyst would spread vanilla sculk through its sculk spreader, so `PinkSculkCatalystBlock` has no block entity: `PinkDeepDark` blooms the nearest one within 8 blocks of any non-player death in the realm and spreads pink sculk there with `PinkSculkPatchFeature.spread`, which is also the biome's (and the cities') patch feature. Their sounds are `ModSounds.PINK_SCULK` (moss, now and then an amethyst tinkle), and two mixins swap the sensor's clicks (`SculkSensorBlock#activate`/`tick`) and the shrieker's shriek (`SculkShriekerBlockEntity#shriek`) for chimes, for the pink blocks only. Textures are vanilla's sculk gradient-mapped (`pink_sculk()` in the texture script); the warden's TransRecolor textures use the same mapping, so every warden in the realm is pink. `PinkSculkParticles` replaces vanilla's shriek, vibration and sonic boom providers with ones that use the pink twin particle types' sprites while the player is in the Trans Realm. Round 12 softens the rest: `ModSounds.soften(SoundEvent)` maps any `entity.warden.*`, `block.sculk*` or `block.calibrated_sculk_sensor*` sound to a soft one of ours (by keyword or suffix, null for anything else), and `ClientPacketListenerMixin` (`handleSoundEvent`, at HEAD) plays the soft one instead whenever the server sends one of those while the player is in the Trans Realm. That catches vanilla sculk blocks, shriekers and anything the two block mixins miss.
- **The pink warden** extends `Warden`: `canTargetEntity` only accepts `Enemy` mobs (and never other wardens), player damage is ignored, `DIG_COOLDOWN` is refreshed every tick so it doesn't dig away, and every 5 seconds it gives players within 20 blocks night vision. `PinkDeepDark` also swaps any darkness on a player in the Trans Realm for night vision (checked every other tick), which covers shriekers too. One is placed by each city centre template and is persistent, like any from a spawn egg or command (`finalizeSpawn`). Wild ones spawn as rare monsters in the biome (one try in eight, none within 96 blocks of another pink warden, no more than two non-persistent ones loaded in the level) and despawn like monsters. Every 5 seconds a pink warden looks for another within 40 blocks; if it should yield (a wild one yields to a guardian, otherwise the higher UUID yields), it stops refreshing `DIG_COOLDOWN` and digs away the way vanilla's wardens leave. That also thins out the persistent wild wardens round 9 let pile up. It speaks softly: it's `setSilent(true)` (so vanilla's own sound calls go nowhere) and overrides `playSound(SoundEvent, float, float)` to play `ModSounds.soften`'s soft version at no more than 0.8 volume. Its twelve `pink_warden_*` sound events (`generate_data.py` `generate_sounds`) are made of vanilla's gentle sounds: amethyst, moss, allay and the like.
- **Sculk people** are `Villager`s of their own entity type, drawn as humanoids. They take the `sculk_person` profession (no job site) on their first tick with 1 xp, so `ResetProfession` never clears it, and reset their offers' uses every 12000 ticks since they can't restock at a job site. Trades: `generate_data.py` `SCULK_PERSON_TRADES`.
- **The ritual.** The city's gate opening is x 13, y 18 to 23, z 11 to 30 in every city centre template, so its middle is the corner (13.5, 21.0, 21.0). The crystal block sits at (7, 20, 20) facing east; its gem is drawn half a block up and half a block to its clockwise side (`SculkRitual.beamOrigin`; the block model's elements, `RitualCrystalBlock`'s shapes and sparkles all use that offset; the item uses an unshifted model), so it floats at (7.5, 21.0, 21.0), and `SculkRitual.aim` is `GATE_DISTANCE` (6) blocks on: the gate's middle. The eight stands ring (7.5, 21.0) on a dais at y 9, within 8 blocks round and 12 below the crystal. `SculkRitual` finds the opening by flood-filling the air in the gate's plane from the air nearest the aimed-at point (up to 5 blocks off, so the first cities' lower crystal still works; more than 400 blocks means a broken frame and nothing happens), takes the opening's bounding box middle as its true centre, and sorts its blocks into square rings out from it. One `RitualPayload` to players within 96 blocks carries everything the client's light show needs (`client/RitualEffects`, drawn with `GlowGeometry`: Fabric's `LevelRenderEvents.COLLECT_SUBMITS` and `submitCustomGeometry`. Since round 12 it uses `RenderTypes.entityTranslucent` on a plain white texture, with full vertex data (colour, UV, no overlay, full-bright light, a normal), and winds every quad to face outward. Round 10's `debugFilledBox()`, from Fabric's own 26.2 render event test, isn't drawn by Iris/BSL. The client also adds our particles along the beams and at the gate (`RitualEffects#sparkle`)); the timeline is `SculkRitual`'s public constants (candle beams every 4 ticks, the crystal wakes at 44, fires at 56, the portal grows one ring every 2 ticks from 61). The beams only show during the ritual (round 14 dropped the steady beam on awake crystals and the sheet of light over the gate), at about half round 13's brightness and with a third of the particles; once the last ring is in, the light lingers `LINGER` (24) ticks, fading over the last `FADE` (14). Active rituals live in memory only (a restart mid-ritual just leaves the candles lit; use the crystal to start it again).
- **The Cloud Realm** is vanilla's floating-islands noise (`tools/vanilla_extra/templates/worldgen/noise_settings_floating_islands.json`) in cloudcite under cloud grass and soil, with one biome (`cloud_isles`), fixed time and no monster spawns. `CloudRealm` builds the arrival cloud and its portal home at (0, 140, 0) whenever the portal block there is missing, keeps a Cloudy there, records each traveller's gate in the `cloud_return` attachment, and every 15 seconds makes each baby happy ghast near a player young again (`setBaby(true)`; vanilla's `setAgeLocked` is protected) and may float a new one in, up to three nearby. White pigs, cows and chickens are data-driven variants whose spawn condition is the Cloud Isles biome.
- **Cloudies** fly by setting their own velocity (like wild fairies). Ridden, the rider's client moves them through vanilla's ridden-entity path: `getRiddenInput` turns forward input into a move along the rider's look (pitch included).
- **Clouds and the beanstalk.** `WoolCloudFeature` ignores its placement height: it puts each cloud 30 to 48 blocks above whatever island is under it (y 150 to 229 over the void), as a big puff with three to seven more heaped round it, each a squashed ball, so clouds are round underneath. `BeanstalkFeature` (one chunk in 400, on an island below y 140) twists two concrete strands round a green core up to 100 blocks, with leaf pads every 8 blocks, and tops it with a bigger cloud (`WoolCloudFeature.cloud`) and a cloud chest of `chests/heavenly_ruin`. Both centre themselves in their chunk so they never write beyond the chunks a feature may touch.
- **Heavenly ruins** are a feature (step 4, one chunk in 9), not a structure, because jigsaw structures can't see whether there's an island under them. `HeavenlyRuinFeature` picks one of the three templates (round 12's `spiral_tower`, `temple` and `sky_shrine`), centres it on its chunk, and only places it where the ground under its four corners is within 3 blocks (`UNEVEN`) of the ground at its middle, `FOUNDATION` (1) block down, turned a random way. Each template's bottom layer is its foundation, its floor level with the ground. Then it shores the ruin up: under every column whose bottom layer isn't air, it fills replaceable blocks (air, plants) with cloudcite, at most `SHORE` (8) deep, so no floor hangs over a dip. The **spiral tower** (13x34x13, x by y by z) is a round tower of gilded cloudcite with gold bands and windows, a holy water pool inside, an outside stair winding once round it to a railed lookout with columns and a stepped holy gold dome and spire. The **temple** (11x11x15) is a columned temple on a stepped platform, one column fallen and a corner of its roof gone, with a holy water pool and an altar. The **sky shrine** (11x13x11) is a fountain under a ring of eight columns and a gold dome. Each has one cloud chest. Their chests (and the beanstalk's) use `chests/heavenly_ruin`: holy gear in three chests in four (40% enchanted, from the enchanting table's list), plus cloud candy, holy water, golden apples, gold and cloud flowers.
- **Holy gear** is vanilla gear with three components on top (`ModItems.holy()`): `UNBREAKABLE`, `ENCHANTMENT_GLINT_OVERRIDE` true, and a lore line. Tools use `HOLY_TOOL_MATERIAL` (diamond's tier, gold's speed and enchantability), armor `HOLY_ARMOR_MATERIAL` (diamond's protection, the `holy_gold` equipment asset). They're never crafted.
- **Holy water** extends `WaterFluid` (as pink lava does `LavaFluid`) and is in `#minecraft:water`, so it swims, drowns, douses and makes sources like water. Its `LiquidBlock` gives anything inside it **Blessed** (12 seconds, refreshed every half second): `BlessedEffect` heals half a heart a second and adds 2 absorption hearts per level (raising `MAX_ABSORPTION`, like vanilla's absorption). Its textures are vanilla water's in gold, with water's alpha, so it's drawn see-through; the client registers its `FluidModel`.
- **Angels** are ambient mobs of the Cloud Isles (weight 12): `Angel.checkAngelSpawnRules` wants the Cloud Realm, light 7 or less, room, one try in three, and no other angel within 32 blocks. They float (no gravity, their own `travel`), drift round where they spawned, turn to look at nearby players, bless hurt players within 6 blocks (or anyone who uses them) with a 40-second cooldown, and fly up when hit. Since round 12 an angel is a glowing orb like the wild fairies (`FairyLightModel`), not a figure: `AngelModel` is an 8-pixel white and gold orb inside a soft golden glow (a see-through shell, `CubeDeformation(1.6)`), a golden octagonal halo turning slowly over it, and two big feathered wings (flight feathers, and coverts behind them, cut out in the texture) beating slowly at its sides. It's drawn `entityTranslucent` and full bright; its box is 0.8 by 1.4 blocks. `angel_texture()` (64x64) lays it out as the model's Javadoc says.
- **The void rescue.** `CloudRescue` cancels `FELL_OUT_OF_WORLD` damage for players in the Cloud Realm (and `IN_WALL` while being carried). A player who isn't flying, riding, gliding, standing or spectating starts drifting down with slow falling (no particles) once they've fallen 6 blocks, and is caught below y 24 over the bottom of the world (`CATCH_Y`) or once they've fallen 10 blocks with nothing under them (`nothingBelow`: the `MOTION_BLOCKING` heightmap, then a scan for anything solid under them). They're put in a new `CloudBoat` (a plain entity drawn with vanilla's boat model and a white texture; vanilla boats are steered by their rider's client, so this one is moved by the server every tick). The boat's type must be saveable: `Entity#startRiding` refuses, on the server, a vehicle whose type can't be saved, which is why round 11's `noSave()` boat never caught anyone. The rescue `claim()`s its boat every tick, and a boat nobody has claimed for 40 ticks (one reloaded after a restart) ejects its rider and discards itself. Since round 14 the rescue runs in stages (`ARRIVE`, `HOOK`, `CARRY`, `LANDED`, `LEAVE`) along planned ways (`Path`: straight legs with their corners rounded off over `CORNER` blocks, walked by distance, with eased speed up to 0.5 blocks a tick). A Cloudy with the `CloudTurtle` sitting on it comes down 30 blocks over 60 ticks to `LINE` (5) blocks above the boat and hooks it. The way home (`wayHome`) first swings out to the nearest open sky if anything hangs over the boat (`openSky`: a 3x3 column with nothing above it, reached by a sideways move that hits nothing), rises to `CLEARANCE` (4) blocks above the highest block along the way (`highestAlong`), crosses, and comes straight down onto the landing spot. That's `nearestLand`: the nearest loaded column within 64 blocks whose top is solid ground (not leaves or a fluid, a sturdy top face, two open blocks above, no neighbour more than a block higher) under open sky, else the arrival cloud. The boat follows the Cloudy's own path `TRAIL` (7) ticks behind, so it swings along on the lead. Landed, he waits (reminding them every 3 seconds, nudging them out after 30) until they get out, then flies up and away through open sky (`wayOff`) and everything vanishes in a puff. A player out of the boat in mid-air for `OVERBOARD` (20) ticks ends the rescue, and a new one catches them. The turtle sits (`CloudTurtleRenderer`: vanilla's riding pose, drawn 0.6 lower), and the Cloudy bobs and turns smoothly. The rescue itself isn't saved: one cut short by a restart just ends (a still-falling player is caught again), the turtle (`noSave`) is gone, the boat discards itself, and the Cloudy stays as an ordinary Cloudy. The client draws the turtle's lead as a striped golden rope, sagging with its slack, from his hand to a sling over the boat (`CloudBoatRenderer`, with `GlowGeometry`).
- **Cloud Chests** (`CloudChestBlock`) are barrels underneath: the block extends `BarrelBlock` (facing the player when placed, never up or down; a chest-sized shape) and `ModBlocks.initialize` adds it to vanilla's `BlockEntityTypes.BARREL` with Fabric's `addValidBlock`, so it keeps vanilla's 27-slot inventory, opening sound and `open` state. The model (`generate_data.py` `generate_round12`) is a chest of white wood (`cloud_chest_{body,lid,top}`, the texture script's `cloud_chest_face`) with golden trim and a holy gold latch; the `open` state lifts the lid. Barrels have no name of their own, so the item carries `CUSTOM_NAME` ("Cloud Chest", not italic), which the placed barrel keeps; the ruins' templates give theirs the same name as a `{translate: ...}` component, and the beanstalk calls `applyComponentsFromItemStack`. Crafted from pale oak planks round a gold ingot.
- **The Fairy Jar's fairy** is a `JarFairy` entity (a tiny `Fairy`: no physics, no trail, invulnerable, unpickable) that the jar makes when placed (`onPlace`) and on its random ticks (for older jars). It drifts to random points inside the glass at about 0.003 blocks a tick and hovers one to four seconds at each, keeps the colour it was rolled (blue, pink or white, saved like a wild fairy's; it no longer follows the jar's clock), sheds a mote in its colour now and then (client side, in place of the jar's old sparkles), and vanishes if the jar is gone. It's drawn cutout at 0.45 scale (a see-through model behind the jar's see-through glass would vanish), with its wings at 0.4 times a wild fairy's beat, and lit at the jar's light level (`FairyJarBlock.LIGHT`, 9) rather than full bright, so shader packs don't blow it out. The jar's block model is the empty jar; its item model keeps the old light.

## Generators

Run from the repository root (needs `pip install pillow nbtlib`), in this order:

1. `python3 tools/generate_textures.py`: textures, `.mcmeta` files, saliva frames, heart clouds, paintings, the advancement background and the icon.
2. `python3 tools/generate_data.py`: blockstates, models, item definitions, loot tables, recipes, block/item/entity/painting tags, painting variants, advancements (it wipes the advancement folder), `sounds.json` and `en_us.json`. It wipes and rewrites `data/minecraft/tags/` except `worldgen`, plus our block and item tags, except those other generators write, listed in `OTHER_GENERATORS_TAGS` (add any new one there, or the next run deletes it).
3. `python3 tools/generate_worldgen.py`: features, biomes (it wipes the biome folder), surface rules, noise settings, the dimension, the dimension type and the timelines. Placed features of all biomes are sorted by one global `FEATURE_RANK` list, because Minecraft crashes on inconsistent feature order. New features must be added there. It also rewrites the realm's own copies of the overworld's density functions and noises (`worldgen/density_function/trans/`, `worldgen/noise/trans/`, from `tools/vanilla_extra/templates/worldgen/overworld/`; see "The realm's own terrain and pink lava").
4. `python3 tools/generate_villages.py`: everything under `structure/village/trans`, `worldgen/template_pool/village/trans`, the processor lists, the village structure and structure set, the village biome tag and `#minecraft:village`.
5. `python3 tools/generate_egg_house.py`: the island template, its pool, structure, structure set, biome tag, map tag, `chests/egg_house` and the map pools (Egg House and Fairy Sanctum maps) in `chests/trans_house`.
6. `python3 tools/generate_fairy_realm.py`: the Fairy Sanctum and arena island templates, the sanctum's structure, pool, structure set, processor list, tags and `chests/fairy_sanctum`.
7. `python3 tools/generate_camps.py`: the trans camp (see "How the camps work"): `structure/trans_camp`, `worldgen/template_pool/trans_camp`, the structure, structure set, biome tag and `chests/trans_camp_{common,secret,barrel}`.
8. `python3 tools/generate_mermaid_ruins.py`: the three mermaid ruins (`structure/mermaid_ruin/`), their pool, the `mermaid_weathering` processor list, the structure, structure set, biome tag and `chests/mermaid_ruin`.
9. `python3 tools/generate_ancient_city.py`: the pink ancient city (see "How the pink deep dark and the Cloud Realm work"): `structure/pink_ancient_city/`, `worldgen/template_pool/pink_ancient_city/`, three `pink_ancient_city_*_degradation` processor lists and their `pink_ancient_city_replaceable` block tag, the structure, structure set, biome tag and `chests/pink_ancient_city(_ice_box)`. Its vanilla inputs (templates, pools, processors, loot) are in `tools/vanilla_extra/`.
10. `python3 tools/generate_heavenly_ruins.py`: the Cloud Realm's heavenly ruins (`structure/heavenly_ruin/`: `spiral_tower`, `temple`, `sky_shrine`, `ruined_church`; it clears the folder first; see "How the pink deep dark and the Cloud Realm work") and `chests/heavenly_ruin` and `chests/ruined_church`. `HeavenlyRuinFeature.FOUNDATION` must match its `FOUNDATION` (1).
11. `python3 tools/validate_resources.py /path/to/mcmeta-summary/registries/data.json`: cross-checks all of the above.

Hand-made files the scripts don't touch: the crystal tools' and armor's item definitions, item models and recipes, `equipment/trans_crystal.json`, the cake models and blockstate, `chests/trans_bakery`, most of `chests/trans_house`, and the baker trades and trade sets.

## Conventions

- Tabs, Javadoc on classes and anything non-obvious, `snake_case` ids in the `transdimension:` namespace.
- Every block needs a blockstate, an item definition (unless registered without an item), a model, a name, a loot table, mining tags and a texture made by the texture script. The validator reports any that are missing.
- Hooks into game internals fail safe: mixins use `require = 0`, and reflection is wrapped in try/catch with a disable flag.
- The owner's photos are not in the repository; only the 48x48 pixelated versions in `tools/art/` are.

## Building in the cloud

The Claude Code cloud environment used so far blocks these hosts, so Gradle can't run there: `maven.fabricmc.net`, `piston-meta.mojang.com`, `piston-data.mojang.com`, `libraries.minecraft.net`, `resources.download.minecraft.net`, `launchermeta.mojang.com`, and for JDK 25 `api.adoptium.net`, `download.java.net` and `cdn.azul.com`. Allowing them under the environment's network access settings (Custom, Allowed domains; see https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cloud-environments#network-access) should make `./gradlew build` possible there. Locally, any JDK 25 works.
